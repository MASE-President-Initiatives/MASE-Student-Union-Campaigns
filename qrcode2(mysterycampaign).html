01 — THE HUMAN BRAIN

Seeing is not quite the same as noticing.

Your eyes take in an enormous amount of visual information. But your brain has to prioritize what deserves your attention. That means the world you consciously experience is only part of what is happening around you.

“We don't experience everything around us. We experience what our minds select.”

A THOUGHT ABOUT PERCEPTION
02 — THINGS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW

Inside your attention.

Three fascinating ways the human mind makes sense of the world.

THE NIGHT SKY — AN INFINITE VIEW
01 / SELECTIVE ATTENTION

Your brain is a very selective editor.

Imagine trying to listen to one friend in a crowded room. There may be dozens of conversations happening around you, but your attention can focus on just one.

This ability helps us concentrate. It also means that things can happen right beside us without entering our conscious awareness.

THE INTERESTING PART Your attention is limited. Focusing intensely on one thing can make other things easier to miss.
A LANDSCAPE — ONE VIEW, MANY DETAILS
02 / CHANGE BLINDNESS

Big changes can go unnoticed.

People can sometimes fail to notice surprisingly large changes in a scene, especially when something interrupts the view between the original scene and the changed one.

This phenomenon is known as change blindness. It shows that our visual experience isn't a perfect, uninterrupted recording of everything we see.

THE INTERESTING PART Even a noticeable change can escape attention if the right distraction occurs at the right moment.
PATTERNS IN THE NATURAL WORLD
03 / PAREIDOLIA

Why do we see faces everywhere?

Have you ever looked at a cloud and seen a face? Or noticed what looks like an expression in the front of a car?

This is called pareidolia: the tendency to perceive familiar shapes, especially faces, in random objects and patterns.

THE INTERESTING PART Your brain is so good at recognizing faces that sometimes it finds them where no face exists.
Sometimes the most fascinating thing isn't what you see. It's how you see it.
03 — A FEW MORE CURIOSITIES

The mind is full of surprises.

Memory isn't a video.

Human memory is reconstructive. Remembering an event involves rebuilding it, and details can change over time.

Context changes perception.

The surroundings of an object can influence how we interpret it. The same thing can look different depending on what is around it.

Quiet has a lot going on.

When the world becomes quieter, sounds that were previously masked can suddenly become noticeable.
